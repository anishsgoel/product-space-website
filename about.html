Georgetown's home for people who build products.

Product Space is a nationwide organization with chapters at Georgetown, UC Berkeley, Columbia and beyond, developing talent and building community around product management. This is the Georgetown chapter: a fellowship that teaches the craft, and client teams that practise it on real products.

The decisions that shape the next decade are being made at technology companies

Where the growth is

The largest companies in the world are technology companies, and AI has pulled enormous capital and attention into the sector. Every major firm is reorganizing around it.

Where the questions are

How AI gets deployed, what it should be allowed to do, and who it serves are being decided right now, inside the companies and startups building it.

Where the reach is

Products built by these teams are used by billions of people. A decision made in a product meeting shows up in how those people work and live.

What is product management?

Product management is the practice of guiding a product from idea through design, launch, and ongoing improvement. It sits between customer needs, business goals, and technology. A product manager decides what to build, and why.

User research

Learn what users actually need and where the product fails them today.

Product strategy

Set the vision and the long-term plan the rest of the work ladders up to.

Roadmapping

Prioritize features and decide what actually gets built next, and what waits.

Team coordination

Work with engineers, designers, and marketers to get the thing shipped.

At the intersection of tech, business, and design

Engineering

Scoping the build, estimating effort, and understanding what is technically possible within the time you have.

Business

Market sizing, unit economics, pricing, and the customer research that tells you whether a thing is worth building at all.

Design

Visual design, UI and UX, user flows, interface decisions, and usability testing against real people.

Product Space runs the full pipeline

No other organization at Georgetown covers the whole sequence, from finding a pain point to shipping against it and revising on real usage data.

  1. 01 · Research

    Find pain points

    User research and interviews to learn where a product actually fails people.

  2. 02 · Strategy

    Prioritize

    Market and competitive analysis, then feature prioritization against real constraints.

  3. 03 · MVP

    Scope and build

    Requirements, wireframes, and prototypes that turn a direction into something testable.

  4. 04 · Iteration

    Define success

    Go-to-market planning and the success metrics that say whether it worked.

  5. 05 · Launch

    Ship and revise

    Usage data and feedback loop back into the roadmap for the next revision.

Two things, done properly

Every new member spends their first semester in the Product Fellowship: twelve weeks of lecture and lab that take you from what a product manager actually does through discovery, business validation, design, metrics, and technical fundamentals. You build a capstone product alongside the curriculum and present it to industry professionals in December.

After that you join a project team working directly with a client company on a real engagement, with a midterm and a final deliverable. Analysts who lead well move up to senior analyst and project manager in later semesters.

Around both of those is a community that goes on a retreat every year, throws parties for Georgetown Day and Homecoming, and plays a lot of Fish.
